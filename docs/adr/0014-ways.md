# ADR 0014: The Ways (Phase 11, first part)

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-01

## Context

After v1.0.0 the owner asked for "virtual journeys" (VISION.md's post-1.0 idea) in the shape of
pilgrim ways, four of them made well, with the lines taken from OpenStreetMap and only the
Italian part of the Via Francigena (PLANNING.md §11 Phase 11, §15). The same conversation ruled
GPS route maps out: a way is a map with no location. Its second part, walks through cities, is
planned on top of this one and not built yet.

## Decisions

1. **The data is written by a script, from OpenStreetMap and Natural Earth.**
   `tools/build_ways.py` reads the ways' route relations through the Waymarked Trails API (which
   hands a relation over in order, with its geometry), keeps the main ways (variants and
   appendices out), joins the few breaks in the mapping (all under 150 m but one of 321 m at the
   gates of Florence), and takes the shortest path from the first stop to the last. The line is
   simplified to under a pixel on the largest phone (466 to 1,003 points a way) and keeps, at
   every point, the metres along the full-resolution line. The content (relations, stops, names,
   one checked sentence for about ten places a way, in English and Italian) is
   `tools/ways_content.py`; the outputs (`WayData.kt`, the place strings, their lookup) are
   never edited by hand. The sources are downloaded by the script's `fetch`, never by the build.
   Only the standard library, like the launcher icon's script.
2. **ODbL, credited where it is read.** The lines are © OpenStreetMap contributors under the
   Open Database License; the derived file says so in its header and is offered under the same
   licence. The credit is on the Ways page, in the guide, in Settings → Credits, in the README
   and in `licenses/`. Land, lakes, rivers and borders are Natural Earth, public domain.
3. **The four, as mapped.** Via degli Dei 123 km (9 stages, the classic towns, and the sanctuary
   of San Luca as a place), Via di Francesco 434 km (22), Camino Francés 768 km (34, and the Cruz
   de Ferro), Via Francigena from the Great St Bernard to Rome 1,020 km (46, and the Cisa Pass).
   A length is the line's own, which is what the point moves along; official figures differ
   between sources anyway. Each stop is snapped to the line, and the build fails if one is more
   than 2 km away or out of order.
4. **The distance is the days', computed on read.** A way keeps where it began and how far it
   has been told, nothing else (`way_journey`, schema v4 by auto-migration). What was walked is
   the sum of the days' own estimated distances from the start: frozen days as they were frozen,
   today as written. History, Insights and the way cannot disagree, and "Apply profile to past
   data" or an import moves a way with everything else.
5. **A finished way stays finished.** Should its days be measured shorter since (a new profile
   applied to the past), the arrival is not taken back: its last stops are reached on the day it
   ended. A way left counts up to the day it was left.
6. **A start in the past, never before the first day counted.** Today, 1 January, the first day
   Passo counted, or a chosen day; a day before the first counted would add nothing and read as a
   date the reader was not walking with Passo, so it is moved to that first day. The start sheet
   says where each choice would place the reader ("you would already be past Vetralla"), and
   offers only the choices that mean different days. One way at a time.
7. **A stage is told by the days being written, not by a timer.** `WayNotifier`, started with
   the process like the goal alarms, watches the days from the journey's start only while a way
   is under way; the tracking service writes them in batches anyway (PLANNING.md §4.5), so a
   stage reached on a walk is told with the batch that wrote it. The stage is claimed in the
   database before it is told (`toldMeters` only moves forward), so two writes close together
   cannot tell it twice; several passed at once are told as the furthest; a start in the past is
   told up to where it places the reader, so the stages behind are stamped, not announced. The
   channel is its own (`ways`), at the default importance, and the reader's to silence. Nothing
   wakes the phone: §9 is unchanged.
8. **The map is drawn in code.** `WayMapView` (`:core:designsystem`), a Canvas like every chart of
   Passo: the ground cut to a square around the frame (so a box of another shape still finds it
   to its edges), the way faint, the part walked in the goal's colour, the stages ahead as rings
   and the ones reached as beads on the line, the reader's point. On the way's page the ends are
   named, the locator (the country, with the way on it) sits in the corner the line leaves most
   free, and a touch names the nearest stop; the small maps keep only the line and its ends. It
   is one image to TalkBack, said in a sentence; the stages are listed in words beside it.
9. **Water is a new colour role.** The dresses put their blue in different roles (Paper's
   secondary, Vivid's primary), and a sea in Vivid's amber did not read as one: `PassoColors`
   gains `water`, a pale sky in light and a deep one in dark, a ground for marks, never for text.
10. **The credential.** A stamp for each stage reached, with its day, set down a little askew,
    each stage with its own tilt and ink; generic stamps, never an official one. A name takes the
    largest size at which it fits three lines with no word cut, breaking only at a space or a
    hyphen.
11. **Places are discovered as they are walked.** A stop's sentence shows once it is reached or
    when it is the next; before a way starts every sentence is there to read.
12. **The backup carries the ways** as an added field (`ways`), with no new format version: an
    older file has none, an older reader skips it. An import adds the file's ways by their start
    instant; one under way comes in under way only if this phone walks none, and otherwise as
    left on the day the file was written.
13. **Where it shows.** The Ways page (from Insights), each way's page, Insights' card for the
    way under way (after the streak) or, with none, the door to the Ways at the end of the page.
    Not on Today, not on the widgets (an open question, §15): the Ways answer a question asked
    weekly, not hourly.

## Consequences

- No new permission, no new dependency, no new wake: the forbidden-permission check and §9 are
  unchanged. The APK grows by about 80 KB of encoded lines.
- City walks (the phase's second part) reuse the script, the data shape, the map and the stamps;
  they bind to outings instead of days.
- To be checked on a device (owner): a backdated start, a stage told on a walk, the maps in both
  themes and on an open foldable.
